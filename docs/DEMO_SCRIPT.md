# AtlasDrive — video script

How to use this script:

- Each line is one breath. Say the line, pause, then the next.
- You do not have to say it word for word. The lines are short so you
  can put them in your own words if that feels better.
- The parts in [brackets] are actions. Do them, don't say them.
- If you trip on a line, just stop and say it again. You will cut it
  in the edit.

---

## 1. The problem

[Start on the Search screen. Don't click anything yet.]

I take photos for a living.

I have about 20 hard drives.

On them are over 200,000 photos.

Weddings. Work jobs. Family stuff. Going back years.

The drives live in a drawer.

And the same thing always happens.

Someone asks for a photo from years ago.

And I think — which drive is it on?

I used to plug in drive after drive, looking for it.

That could take hours.

So I made this app.

It's called AtlasDrive.

It knows what is on every drive.

Even when the drive is not plugged in.

And it all runs on my Mac.

No cloud. Nothing gets uploaded.

The app never changes my photos. It only reads them.

## 2. Search

[Type "cocktail" in the search box. Press Search.]

Watch this. I'll type... cocktail.

[Point at the "Found on Drive 2" box]

And there's my answer.

Drive 2. It found 44 photos.

So now I know which drive to get out of the drawer.

[Scroll down to the photo thumbnails]

I can see the photos right away.

The drive does not need to be plugged in for this.

Each photo shows me its drive, its folder, and its date.

If the drive is plugged in, one click opens the real file.

And this button here finds photos that look like it.

## 3. Browse

[Clear the search box. Point at the word chips.]

Sometimes you don't know what to type.

So the app shows you what is in your photos.

It worked all this out on its own.

Over 8,000 photos with people in them.

[Click one chip — "wedding"]

I click one... and there they are.

[Click a second chip — "child"]

I click one more... and it narrows down.

Now it's only photos with both things in them.

[Click a drive chip — e.g. Drive 3]

And I can look at just one drive if I want.

[Point at the "Find names in photographs" note]

One more thing. This bit I love.

The app can read words inside the photos.

A bottle behind a bar. A sign. A van.

So I can search for a name — like a gin brand.

And it finds every photo where that name shows up.

## 4. Drives

[Click Drives in the side bar]

This is where my drives live.

Each drive gets a number.

The same number I write on the label.

I can also note where it is kept. Like — drawer 2.

[Point at a drive card]

For each drive, it shows what is on it.

And this line here is the one I trust.

"Finished — safe to unplug."

So when a scan runs all night, I know when it's done.

One button starts a scan. One button stops it.

If I stop half way, nothing is lost.

It just picks up where it left off next time.

## 5. Watching a scan

[Click Scan activity]

This is a scan running.

I can see how fast it's reading.

How many photos it has found.

And when it thinks it will finish.

If it can't read a file, it tells me.

In plain words. Not error codes.

And one button lets it try those files again.

## 6. People

[Click People]

Every face it finds ends up here.

From every drive.

I name someone once.

After that, the app spots them on every new drive.

It only ever suggests. I say yes or no.

I can also mark people as family.

So the people I care about most have their own filter.

All the face data is locked up on my Mac.

## 7. Events

[Click Events]

The app also sorts photos into events.

It looks at when the photos were taken.

So a wedding shows up as one event.

I don't have to remember folder names any more.

## 8. The end

[Go back to the Search screen]

One last thing.

When a scan ends, the app backs up its records.

The backup goes to my Google Drive.

But the photos never leave my machines.

So that's AtlasDrive.

20 drives. 200,000 photos. One search box.

The drives stay in the drawer.

Plug in. Scan. Unplug. Done.

And every drive I add makes the search better.

[Stop recording]

## Short version — 30 seconds

I have 20 hard drives and 200,000 photos.

The question that used to eat my time was — which drive is it on?

[Type a search]

Now I just type what I remember.

Drive 2. 44 photos. There they are.

The drive can stay in the drawer.

It reads faces. It sorts events. It can even read names on bottles.

All on my Mac. Nothing uploaded.

It's called AtlasDrive.
